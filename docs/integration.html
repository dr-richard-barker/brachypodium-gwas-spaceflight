Cross-Species Spaceflight Conservation (Monocot vs Dicot)

Figure 4. Cross-species meta-analysis connecting 2,550 consensus Arabidopsis thaliana spaceflight response genes (17 NASA GeneLab studies) with Brachypodium distachyon (OSD-375), highlighting significant cross-species conservation of core stress and metabolic pathways (Fisher's exact test p = 0.0446, Odds Ratio = 13.70).

Conserved vs Monocot-Specific Spaceflight Responses

Our comparative analysis identifies both broadly conserved and grass-specific adaptations to orbital microgravity:

  • Conserved Core Responses: Reactive oxygen species (ROS) detox peroxidases, heat shock factors (HSFs), photosynthetic light reactions, and hypoxia adaptation.
  • Monocot-Specific Responses: Extensive remodeling of grass-specific Type II cell walls (mixed-linkage β-glucans and ferulic acid cross-linking) and organ-specific auxin redistribution networks.

Mechanistic Model: Gravity Perception in 1g vs Microgravity

Figure 5. Comparative biophysical model of monocot gravity perception. (A) Under terrestrial 1g, starch statolith sedimentation triggers LZY translocation to the plasma membrane (Nishimura et al. 2023, Science), directing polar PIN auxin transport and asymmetric expansin cell wall loosening. (B) Under ISS microgravity, statolith suspension activates mechanosensitive Ca2+ channels (BdCPK28, BdCAS, BdMSL10) and compensatory BdLAZY1/BdPIN3 transcription alongside Type II cell wall remodeling.
